## Design Context

### Users

Drivers and retro-instrument enthusiasts use Anode as a glanceable GPS
speedometer and as a focused design tool for authoring VFD instrument faces.
The runtime must remain safe in a windshield mount; the editor must support
touch, pointer, phones, tablets, and resizable iPad windows.

### Brand Personality

Mechanical, photographic, uncompromising. Anode should feel like operating
1980s automotive switchgear and viewing a physical vacuum fluorescent module,
not a modern mobile UI wearing a retro theme.

### Aesthetic Direction

Near-black VFD substrate, photograph-tuned cyan-green/amber/red phosphor,
smoked acrylic Prism controls, Barlow Condensed switch legends, hard detents,
latches, and direct tactile motion. No visible Material controls, kinetic
content scrolling, generic glassmorphism, or decorative modern UI chrome.

### Design Principles

1. Preserve optical physics and the one-pass VFD renderer over conventional
   visual cleanliness.
2. Make state and navigation mechanical: explicit controls, hard stops,
   detents, and recoverable actions.
3. Keep runtime interaction deliberate and driver-safe; keep editing precise,
   accessible, and non-destructive.
4. Keep physical faces a fixed authored extent, measured in frame-independent
   design units. Opposite viewports contain the primary unchanged unless an
   explicit alternate was authored; never rotate or reflow.
5. Adapt editor chrome to window shape without changing persisted design data,
   and preserve 44 px touch targets, keyboard access, and reduced motion.
6. Treat layout editing as direct mechanical calibration: absolute centre and
   size, session-only SNAP detents, screen-space handles, and one compact PLACE
   surface. Never let preview orientation choose service-bay geometry.
7. Keep LOOK approachable: expose phosphor and one TUNE latch, then reveal one
   labelled optical channel at a time behind a fixed-footprint service hatch.
   HVAC levers snap their thumb and stored value to the same visible detents;
   pictograms are secondary marks, never unlabeled navigation.
